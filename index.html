How the model decides

From the shop floor to the boardroom

  1. 01

    Operating assumptions

    Shifts × days × productive hours × operators = addressable labor hours.

  2. 02

    Realized savings

    Hours × loaded cost × realization × uptime — only cash that can genuinely be released.

  3. 03

    Free cash flow

    Benefits − maintenance, service and electricity − cash tax + after-tax salvage.

  4. 04

    NPV · IRR · payback

    Five annual cash flows discounted at the required return.

  5. 05

    Decision gate

    Economics, payload, throughput and 13 QA checks combine into invest, pilot or retain.

Recommendation

Run a pilot before committing capital

The rule says INVEST NOW. The uncertainties are empirical, not mathematical — so the next step is evidence.

  1. Firm quoteInstalled-cell scope, warranty and service.
  2. Time and cycle studyOne shift with the site's cartons, layout and pallet pattern.
  3. Measure the cellSustained throughput, uptime, stoppages and operator intervention.
  4. Confirm labor releaseGenuine cost avoidance or measurable productive capacity.
  5. Rerun and approveReplace provisional inputs, rerun the downside, seek owner approval.

Limitations of the model
  • The company and operating case are hypothetical.
  • Capex and recurring costs are placeholders pending supplier and integrator quotes.
  • Uptime, cycle rate, line demand and labor release require site measurement.
  • The cash-tax calculation is simplified and gives no tax benefit for negative incremental EBIT.
  • Financing effects, safety, quality and incremental-capacity benefits are excluded.
  • Payload and throughput checks do not replace a risk assessment or integration study.
